The present study tests four hypotheses. 
First, we will examine how attentional/impulse-control dysregulation, externalizing behaviour, and internalizing difficulties evolve over time and interact. We expect each construct to show temporal stability, such that higher levels at one time point predict higher levels of the same construct at subsequent time points. We also expect the three constructs to covary within each measurement wave. However, we remain agnostic about the direction and magnitude of the cross-lagged associations between those constructs over time.
Second, we will investigate whether distinct adversity dimensions at age 3 and age 5 (threat, deprivation, and two forms of environmental uncertainty, namely stochasticity and volatility) predict initial deviations in behavioural regulation at age 3 and age 5. We generally expect greater exposure to adversity to be associated with more severe psychopathology. However, we do not formulate predictions regarding the specific effects of each adversity dimension on each of the three psychopathology constructs. The inclusion of these environmental predictors will make it possible to test the structure of the developmental cascade.
Third, we will test whether mid-adolescent psychopathology states, together with stable psychopathology liabilities, are associated with differential investment in mating effort and risky health behaviours, and whether investment in these two domains is itself related. This approach is consistent with the idea that psychopathology may influence how individuals regulate trade-offs between fitness-relevant strategies. For this hypothesis, we specifically expect the strongest associations to involve motivational outcomes and externalising psychopathology scores. For the remaining associations, we remain agnostic about their direction and magnitude. 
Fourth, we will investigate the way adversity dimensions propagate their effect throughout the development until the 15-year-old outcomes. 

By integrating dimensional models of adversity, developmental cascade frameworks, evolutionary life-history theory, and Bayesian longitudinal modelling, the study aims to clarify the developmental pathways through which early conditions calibrate behavioural motivations across development, illustrating how developmental psychopathology and evolutionary theory jointly explain the calibration of human behavioural strategies.
